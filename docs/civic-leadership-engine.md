# Civic leadership engine

Direct Democracy treats leadership as a public record of useful civic practice, not as a popularity contest or a single reputation score.

## Product model

Leadership evidence is earned inside contextual civic rooms:

- issues hold sourced concerns, experience, questions, evidence, proposals, and counterpoints
- debates hold ordered arguments and direct responses
- events hold participation around a specific public purpose
- petitions hold an explicit request and a path toward sponsorship or action
- cases and public records hold contributions tied to inspectable civic context

The profile assembles evidence from those rooms. It does not create a separate global posting feed.

## Dimensions

The first release shows five independent dimensions:

- Grounded knowledge: contributions tied to context, evidence, claims, or citations
- Constructive deliberation: participation in structured debate
- Civic initiative: debates, events, and petitions started
- Follow-through: work carried to a completed or progressed state
- Community regard: specific acknowledgments received from other participants

Each dimension is descriptive and displays its underlying signal count. The states `Starting`, `Building`, and `Demonstrated` help a person see where their public record is growing without collapsing the record into a universal number.

## Acknowledgments

Debate readers can attach one acknowledgment to another person's contribution:

- Well sourced
- Helped me understand
- Fair representation
- Constructive challenge
- Practical proposal

Acknowledgments are distinct from support or opposition. A person should be able to recognize the civic value of an argument without agreeing with its conclusion.

## Guardrails

- Followers and raw reactions do not determine the leadership stage.
- People cannot acknowledge their own debate contribution.
- Acknowledgments are removable and limited to one per reader per contribution.
- The record is not an endorsement, candidate ranking, eligibility decision, or measure of personal worth.
- The current prototype uses cookie-backed interaction state. Durable identity, moderation, and abuse controls are required before acknowledgments influence consequential access or privileges.

## Next releases

1. Add structured contribution types consistently to events and petitions.
2. Add commitments with owners, due dates, completion evidence, and outcome updates.
3. Let residents nominate issue stewards with written reasons and cross-perspective support.
4. Add moderation review and anomaly detection for coordinated acknowledgment behavior.
5. Add a finite Civic Briefing that routes users to unanswered questions, open commitments, upcoming decisions, and outcomes from rooms they joined.
